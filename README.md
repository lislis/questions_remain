# Views on a Lake

The interactive audio installation “Views on a Lake” explores the various residents of Lake Seddin through the feelings of a fictitious self and locates them on a map. The different needs of the human and non-human residents are not always understood. Often they are at odds with each other. Together they paint a multi-layered picture of a social ecosystem and its problems.

## Context

"Views on a Lake" was created for the exhibition concluding the artistic research seminar "The Lake Vanishes" at Filmuniversity Babelsberg Konrad Wolf. The subject of the seminar and its research was the nearby Lake Seddin in Brandenburg, Germany, which is losing water, slowly vanishing.

## Intention and ideation

For me the most interesting part of the "Seddin story" was how diverse the circumstances of the surrounding ecosystem are. Different human social classes, animals and plants are in conflict with each other, in a sense fighting for the lake's resources.

The human factors influence the lake the most, the animals and plants have to deal with the fallout. Some of the human factors are quite obvious, others are hidden and require special insights. I think this is the main reason for a lack of empathy towards the different stakeholders, just not knowing about the situation.

So I wanted to paint a picture to give an overview of all that and, quite literally, paint a picture. The idea was to use a map, which is generally considered neutral and based on reality and data and therefore by default correct and true, but hand-paint it, inevitably introducing error and subjectivity. I think this dichotomy reflects the positions surrounding the lake quite well.

Personally, I like interactive and non-linear pieces, so this would be no exception. This is how I ended up with a painted canvas, wired up on the backside for certain touch points, and a pair of headphones attached. The touch-sensitive points of interest (POI) on the canvas trigger sound snippets that can be heard through the headphones. Spectators can choose the order of the POI and if they want to listen to all of them or only a few.

Touching things gives us an added sensation that can stay with us and help connect memories and thoughts, which I found would help creating something more memorable. The painting uses a real, structured canvas with a nice feel. I then painted wide areas in water color, and details in acrylic paint (I have no background in fine arts and a limited budget), offering different textures.

The POI are drawn onto the canvas with a back felt pen, next to each is a small LED. On the backside the POI are pieces of tin foil connected to micro-controllers to read the touch.  When people touch these point the corresponding LED ligths up to give immediate feedback, and an audio file is played through the headphones.
